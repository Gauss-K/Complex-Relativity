# Complex Relativity (CR)

Complex Relativity is a developing research framework exploring the unification of gravity and matter through complex spacetime and a shared light cone.

## Foundations

CR starts from two physical postulates:

- **P1 — Real observers and causal geometry:** Physical observers use a real Lorentzian geometry whose light cone is shared by the complex metric.
- **P2 — Real momentum and mass shells:** Physical momenta and masses remain real, with mass-shell constraints compatible with the complex formulation.

Complex spacetime and the light cone provide the mathematical and physical foundations.

## Initial Publications

The repository begins with four complementary documents:

### CR Full Theory

A comprehensive presentation of the framework, covering its postulates, complex spacetime geometry, gravitational dynamics, and response sectors. It develops the mathematical formulation and distinguishes established derivations within the models from additional assumptions, proposed interpretations, and unresolved questions.

### Introduction to CR

A pedagogical introduction for advanced physics undergraduates and graduate students. It develops the central ideas through physical explanations, worked calculations, and exercises, connecting the formal framework with familiar concepts from relativity and field theory.

### English Popular Guide

An accessible booklet for general readers. Everyday analogies, intuitive explanations, and a light, humorous style introduce the central ideas without assuming specialist mathematical training.

### Chinese Popular Guide

An expanded Chinese adaptation for general readers, with additional explanations and examples tailored to a Chinese-speaking audience. It offers a more leisurely exploration of the physical ideas, their motivations, and the questions that remain open.

These documents will be revised and supplemented as the research develops.

## Research Scope

Current research examines classical gravitational dynamics, interacting response sectors parameterized by **C**, field-space curvature, scattering amplitudes, and quantum effective field theory.

The aim is to establish calculable relationships between geometry and physical interactions, while distinguishing derived results from additional assumptions.

## Current Status

CR remains an evolving research programme. Its connection to observed particle content, its extension to higher energies, and distinctive experimental predictions remain open questions.
